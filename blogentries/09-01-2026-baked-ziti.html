baked ziti recipe

Hi I wanted to have a place to keep some recipes, I'm not exactly a cook so the recipes I will list are ones that I haven't created myself. This recipe is for tomato sauce (I think bolognese sauce? I'm not exactly sure on the exact name it's an italian meat sauce) and baked ziti that I took from my parents. I've only made it once on my own so we're learning together. I'd just like to have the recipe on my website to make it easier for me to access, as it's just in my journal. I don't have ANY photos of the process and a terrible photo of the final process. So next time I make it I'll try and make a point to take photos for here.


The Sauce

I am SO sorry in advance because I will be mentioning specific brands, and depending on where you are, or just like. Literally any other multitude of reasons, you might not be able to get the exact brand. Bottom line is if you can afford it, splurge a little on the cheese and tomato sauce. Or for the sauce I guess be more particular, like get a brand where you can look at the can and trust it'll taste good. (terrible guideline but whatever...) The rest of the stuff I don't think matters as much get whatever brand you want.


Ingredients

  • - 2 cans (each 28oz) Tuttorosso Tomato Sauce
  • - 1lb ground beef (you can use sausage but idk i haven't tried doing that. meat is optional but it definitely adds to it)
  • - Extra virgin olive oil
  • - Locatelli Pecorino Romano (this one is like hard to find but like. it's a must have in my mind)
  • - 1 clove of garlic (minced)
  • - Basil (fresh basil recommended if you have a plant or garden)
  • - Oregano
  • - Parsley
  • - Black pepper

1. Pour olive oil and minced clove of garlic into the pot. The olive oil should make a pool in the center that covers roughly 70-80% of it? It's not very particular last time I made it the oil made a thin layer across the bottom of the pot and I guess it was ok. (There's probably reasons why it wasn't ok but I don't know them yet) You want a decent sized pot. I don't know how pot sizes work just big enough to hold two cans of tomato sauce and half a can of water. (preview for later)


2. Turn the burner to medium and cook until the garlic is golden brown and sizzling. In the meantime get your ground beef ready.


3. Put the ground beef in. We are browning ground beef. I can give a small rundown here but I am just stealing everything from this article by amanda from amanda's cooking. I would follow everything here, and don't worry about burning the garlic when you turn the heat up to high. It will be fine. I'm also pretty sure you don't have to brown the beef in the same pot, you can use a separate pan, and it might?? end up better since the pot has too much fat in it with the oil? But I'm not sure. Maybe that can be an experiment. If you're cooking sausage you're the professional here I'm not going to go over how to cook sausage because I don't know how amanda didn't tell me.

  • Put the ground beef in the pot (or pan) as chunks. Use your hands. I think I would recommend 93% lean ground beef if you're using the pot with the oil, since you already have so much fat. Then 80% lean if you're cooking it alone. But it's up to you. I think amanda says to use a little bit of neutral oil if you are cooking 93% lean beef. Olive oil isn't a neutral oil so we're letting amanda down. I don't even know what a neutral oil is. (It turns out fine enough if you cook it in the pot)
  • Maybe stir things around a bit now to get the garlic out from under.
  • Flatten the ground beef chunks into little patties using a spatula.
  • Turn the heat up to high, and let them sit for 4-5 minutes. (approx.) Literally do not touch anything it'll mess with the browning process you just wait.
  • When that's done, you flip every chunk over.
  • On the other side, cook for another 3-4 minutes. (approx.)
  • Then when it's browned on both sides then you break up the beef into tinier pieces.
  • When it's all broken down, this is where I recommend you add some black pepper. It's all too taste if you put too much in the recipe then just do less next time we live and learn. I think be kind of generous with the amount you use though.


    • 4. After your beef (or other meat) is finished and you let amanda down, then you can pour in the two cans of Tuttorosso Tomato Sauce. Save at least one of the cans.


    5. Now, you can fill up one of the cans halfway with water and pour it into the sauce. This is just to make the sauce thinner, I've always done it with water and I'm not sure how thick the sauce is when you skip this step. But this ultimately is all up to you, you can try with more or less water if you want. Or no water at all. I do the recipe with a half a can though. Be sure to still hold on to one can, it's just nice to keep your ladle in for the stirring step later.


    6. Add the Pecorino Romano. Take a tablespoon and take 5 heaping spoonfuls from the container and put it in the sauce. Like heaping. Stir a bit after.


    7. Add the rest of your seasoning, (preferably fresh) basil, oregano, parsley, and black pepper. All to taste, but I think don't be afraid to put a lot you're making a lot of sauce. Sorry if me saying this made you put too much in.


    8. Mix & cook until almost boiling, just until it's boiling. The stove should still be on hot while doing this, then once it reaches that point turn it down to low. Low for me is around 2-3 out of 10 on the dial, and make sure to use the SMALL burner for this not the big one. Like you're turning it to low on the small burner when previously it was on high on the large burner.


    9. So this recipe works on its own as a sauce recipe for whatever you want. So you have a choice here.

    If you are making baked ziti, cook for 2 hours.

    If you are just making sauce, cook for 2.5-3 hours

    You are stirring every 15-20 minutes. For the entire cooking time. It's nice to have a can to keep the ladle in in-between stirring. Do not let it bubble too high, if it looks like it is, just turn it down a little bit. Then turn it back up if you feel it isn't enough. Just keep it around that 2.5/10-3/10 range. It's not that bad to nurse just don't lose track of time doing whatever else. You also will have time to prepare other things (such as the baked ziti) for the latter end of the cooking time. You also have time to plan all of this so be efficient.


    That's it for the sauce! This makes a ton, like even more than you will end up using in the baked ziti, so definitely store it for later. The ingredients come out to a lot but you're making dinner for yourself for like the entire week it's so much food.
